### Disney BRDF Relevant source files: **warp.h, warp.cpp, disney_bsdf.cpp** Following the recomended paper we implement the Disney Principled BRDF. We implement the parameters: metallic, specular, roughness, sheen and clearcoat. This means that we implement 4 "lobes", namely 1)Diffuse lobe, 2)Specular lobe, 3)Clearcoat lobe, 4)Sheen lobe. Since sheen is a minor contribution, during sampling we take into account only the first three lobes and we use the following weights: $$ w_{\text{diffuse}} = 1 - \text{metallic}, w_{\text{specular}} = 1, w_{\text{clearcoat}} = 0.25*\text{clearcoat} $$ which we normalize to convert to probabilities. For the diffuse term we perform cosine sampling and for the other two we importance sample the D term of the microfacet model. Below we present 5 rows corresponding from top to bottom to metallic, specular, roughness, sheen and clearcoat. The corresponding value goes from 0.2(left) to 1.0 with steps of 0.2. All other attributes are set to 0.5 each time.
More specifically the D term for the specular lobe is the GTR2 distribution while for the clearcoat is the GTR1 distribution. We modify the wraptest executable to show samples from these two distributions and for the whole Disney BRDF. ## Participating Media Relevant source files: **phase.h, medium.h, ray.h, scene.cpp, shape.h, multiscatblmedium.cpp, singlescatblmedium.cpp, htmultiscatmedium.cpp htsinglescatmedium.cpp, linmultiscatmedium.cpp** We take inspiration from both the pbrt book and the article of Fong et al. More specifically we implement the new interfaces PhaseFunction and Medium. The Medium is attached to a shape and a PhaseFunction is attached to the Medium. The main path tracing algorithm is almost the same except that in the case of the first intersection with a Medium Boundary Surface we call the integrate method of this Medium to give the chance to the ray to stop at a point inside the Medium(instead of passing through). We also augment the ray struct to keep track of the mediums that it is in and the intersection with a flag indicating if its a surface interaction or a medium one. We implemented the simple isotropic function to handle the in scattering and we used light sampling for direct lighting inside the medium. ### Homogeneous Medium We implement both multiple scattering and single scattering versions for the homegenous medium. ## Emissive Participating Media Relevant source files: **emissive_multiscatblmedium.cpp, emissive_htmultiscatmedium.cpp** In order to handle the emmisive part of the media we modify the previous methods to randomly return the Emitted radiance at the sampled medium point. More specifically, equation 16 of Fong et al states: $$ L_x = \int_{t=0}^d p(t)[\frac{\sigma_a}{\sigma_t}L_e + \frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_t}L_s]dt $$ where p(t) is the distribution according to which we sample the step $t$. So far we had $L_e=0$ and each time we ended up in a point in $[0,d]$ we updated the throughput by multiplying it with the scattering albedo and continued towards direct lighting calculation. If the medium is emmisive, we can insted decide which term to compute based on the ratios $\frac{\sigma_a}{\sigma_t}$ and $\frac{\sigma_s}{\sigma_t}$. Since they are perfectly importance sampled we proceed as follows: we sample a uniform value, if the result is less than the scattering albedo we proceed to compute the direct lighting at the medium point(without updating the throughput). Otherwise we return the Emitted radiance at this point and terminate the path. This method collects radiance only in points that belong to the path and essentially is the direct lighting method based on material sampling. The null scattering algorithm that pbrtv4 uses also collects radiance only from points in the path. For this reason this method is not as efficient as Next Event Estimation and produces noisy results especially when the emissive media is thin.
